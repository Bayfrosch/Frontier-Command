# Frontier Command - Gameplay Rules

## Purpose

This document defines the first concrete gameplay rules for Frontier Command.

These values are not final balance values. They exist to make the first playable prototype consistent, testable, and measurable.

---

# Match Settings

## Target Match Duration

A standard match should last:

* Short match: 20-40 minutes
* Standard match: 0.5-2 hours
* Long match: 2+ hours

The default design target is:

**1.5 hours**

---

## Player Counts

Initial support should focus on:

* 1v1
* 2v2
* Free-for-all

---

## Map Sizes

Map size should scale with player count.

Recommended initial sizes:

* Small 1v1: 128 x 128 tiles
* Standard 1v1: 192 x 192 tiles
* 2v2: 256 x 256 tiles
* Large team map: 320 x 320 tiles

A tile represents a logical gameplay unit, not necessarily one rendered pixel.

---

# Starting Conditions

Each player starts with:

* 1 Command Structure
* 1 Construction Unit
* 1 Scout Unit
* 15000 Materials

Starting units should allow players to:

* Begin base construction
* Start army construction
* Scout the map

---

# Resources

Frontier Command uses two main resources:

## Materials

Used for:

* Buildings
* Units
* Research
* Defenses

## Energy

Energy is a fixed power-capacity resource. It is not accumulated or spent.

Power Plants provide Energy capacity and powered buildings require Energy capacity.

```text
Available Energy = Total Energy Production - Total Energy Requirement
```

Power state rules:

* If total production is equal to or greater than total requirement, all buildings receive power.
* If total requirement exceeds total production, the player enters low power.
* Units do not require Energy unless explicitly stated.
* Destroying or disabling a Power Plant immediately updates the player's available Energy.
* Cancelling or destroying that building releases the reserved Energy.

Initial low-power rule:

* Production and research buildings pause.
* Defensive structures stop firing.
* Detection structures stop detecting hidden units.
* Construction continues at 50% speed.
* Power Plants and Command Structures remain operational.

---

# Income Rules

## Materials

Materials are gathered from resource fields by harvesters.

Base rule:

* 1 Harvester carries 1000 Materials per trip
* 1 full gather cycle takes approximately 30 seconds

A starting economy should support basic production but not rapid expansion and research at the same time.

---

## Energy

Energy is generated by Energy Structures.

Base rule:

* Basic Generator provides 10 Energy capacity.
* Advanced Generator provides 25 Energy capacity.

Energy should become increasingly important in mid and late game.

---

# Resource Depletion

Material fields should be finite.

Starting resource field: 200,000 Materials

Later on every faction should unlock a relyable way of producing unlimited material.

Energy does not deplete, but requires infrastructure investment.

# Research Rules

Research should be expensive enough to require commitment.

## Research Tiers

### Tier 1 - Core Research

* Cost: 1000-1500 Materials, 15-30 Energy
* Time: 45-90 seconds
* Purpose: basic improvements and unlocks

### Tier 3 - Doctrine Research

* Cost: 1500-3500 Materials, 50 Energy
* Time: 120-180 seconds
* Purpose: unlock powerful units, buildings, and systems

Research should compete directly with army production.

### Fleet size Research

* Cost: fix + upkeep costs over time 500->5000 Materials, 10 Energy + 5%->50% of Material income

# Production and Technology Rules

## Prerequisites

Every unit, building, and research project has an explicit list of prerequisites.

Initial prerequisite chain:

1. Command Structure unlocks Construction Units, Basic Generators, Refineries, and Barracks.
2. Barracks unlocks Infantry, Scouts, and Envoy Units.
3. Refinery unlocks Harvesters.
4. Basic Generator and Barracks unlock the Vehicle Factory.
5. Basic Generator and Vehicle Factory unlock the Air Field and Capital Factory.
6. Basic Generator and one basic production structure unlock Research Center (Army and Civilian).

Losing a prerequisite does not destroy or disable already completed units and buildings. It prevents starting new dependent production or research until the prerequisite is restored.

## Production Queues

* Each production structure has one queue.
* A queue may contain up to 99 items.
* The first queued item is produced; later items wait in order.
* Different production structures operate in parallel.
* Completed ground units appear at the structure's exit and move toward its rally point.

## Payment, Cancellation, and Destruction

* The full Material cost is paid when production or research starts.
* An item cannot start if its cost, prerequisites, or fleet-cap requirement are not met.
* Cancelling an unfinished unit or research project refunds 100% of its Material cost.
* Cancelling a waiting queue item refunds 100%.
* If the producing structure is destroyed, the active item is lost without a refund.
* Waiting items are cancelled and refunded at 100% when their producing structure is destroyed.
* Paused production retains its current progress.

## Fleet Size

* Every combat unit consumes fleet capacity.
* Workers and Harvesters initially consume no fleet capacity.
* A unit reserves its capacity when production begins.
* Destroyed or cancelled units immediately release their reserved capacity.
* Existing units remain active if the player's fleet-cap limit is reduced below current usage.
* No new unit may begin production until usage is at or below the limit.

## Rally Points

* Production structures may have one rally point.
* A rally point may target a valid position.
* Newly produced units receive a move command toward that target.

---

# Construction Rules

## Construction Units

Construction Units build all structures.

Rules:

* Buildings can be placed anywhere on valid terrain.
* Construction Unit must travel to the build location.
* Construction begins when the Construction Unit reaches the site.

---

# Movement Rules

## Movement Categories

Units use one of these movement categories:

* Ground
* Hover
* Air

Ground units follow traversable terrain and cannot cross blocked terrain. Hover units may cross specified shallow-water and rough-terrain areas. Air units ignore ground obstacles but remain inside map boundaries.

## Movement Execution

* Movement speed is defined per unit.
* Units accelerate and decelerate rather than changing speed instantly.
* Units rotate toward their movement direction using their defined turn rate.
* A move command completes when the unit enters its arrival radius.
* Units slow down near their destination to reduce overshooting and congestion.
* A unit that cannot reach the exact destination moves to the nearest reachable point.
* If no valid path is found after three attempts, the command fails and the Client receives an unreachable-destination event.

## Collision and Separation

* Friendly ground units use local avoidance to prevent overlap.
* Units may gently push friendly units that are idle or moving in the same direction.
* Units cannot push enemy units, buildings, or immobilized units.
* Large units receive higher avoidance priority than small units.
* Units must not become permanently trapped by friendly units.
* Air units do not collide with ground units.

## Group Movement and Formations

* A group move command assigns positions around the destination.
* Faster units reduce speed when necessary to keep the group reasonably together.
* The initial prototype uses a simple rectangular formation.
* Units select formation positions appropriate to their size and movement category.
* Units may leave formation temporarily to avoid obstacles or engage targets.
* Formation movement must not prevent the player from issuing individual commands.

## Movement During Combat

* A unit may fire while moving only if its weapon allows it.
* Weapons with a stationary-fire requirement prevent movement during their firing sequence.
* An attack-move command engages valid enemies encountered along the route.
* After an attack-move target is lost or destroyed, the unit resumes its route.
* A direct attack command may pursue its target up to the unit's pursuit distance.
* When the pursuit distance is exceeded, the unit returns to its previous position or command.

---

# Repair Rules

Construction Units can repair buildings and mechanical units.

Base rule:

* Repair restores 40 health per second.
* Repair costs 25% of the original unit or building cost over a full repair.
* Repair can be interrupted by damage.
* Repairing under fire is possible but inefficient.

---

# Combat Rules

## Core Formula

Damage is calculated as:

```text
Final Damage = Base Damage x Armor Modifier - Base Defence
```

Minimum final damage is always 1.

Calculation rules:

* Armor Modifier is selected from the weapon's modifier against the target's armor class.
* Base Defence is flat damage reduction defined by the target.
* Multiplication is applied before Base Defence.
* Fractional final damage is rounded to the nearest whole number.
* Damage modifiers from veterancy, abilities, and status effects are applied to Base Damage before the armor calculation.
* A destroyed target cannot receive further damage.

Initial armor modifier matrix:

| Weapon Class | Light | Medium | Heavy | Structure | Air | Capital |
| ------------ | ----: | -----: | ----: | --------: | --: | ------: |
| Small Arms   |  1.25 |   0.75 |  0.40 |      0.30 | 0.00 |    0.25 |
| Cannon       |  1.00 |   1.00 |  0.85 |      0.75 | 0.00 |    0.65 |
| Anti-Armor   |  0.75 |   1.25 |  1.50 |      1.00 | 0.00 |    1.25 |
| Explosive    |  1.25 |   1.00 |  0.75 |      1.25 | 0.00 |    0.75 |
| Anti-Air     |  0.25 |   0.25 |  0.25 |      0.10 | 1.50 |    0.50 |

A modifier of `0.00` means that the weapon cannot target that armor class.

---

## Attack Timing

Each weapon defines:

* Wind-up time
* Cooldown
* Base Damage
* Accuracy
* Minimum and maximum range
* Valid target types
* Projectile or instant-hit behavior

Initial rules:

* A weapon begins its cooldown after firing.
* A weapon cannot fire again until its cooldown completes.
* Cancelling an attack during wind-up cancels the shot.
* Movement interrupts wind-up for weapons that require the unit to remain stationary.

---

## Projectiles and Instant-Hit Weapons

* Instant-hit weapons resolve accuracy and damage when they fire.
* Projectile weapons create a projectile after the firing wind-up completes.
* A projectile aimed at a unit follows that target only if it is defined as guided.
* An unguided projectile continues toward the target's position at firing time.
* Guided projectiles are destroyed if their target becomes invalid unless the weapon defines retargeting.
* A projectile that reaches its target resolves its impact during the current simulation tick.
* Projectiles cannot exist outside map boundaries.

---

## Area Damage and Friendly Fire

* Area damage checks targets within the weapon's impact radius.
* Direct-hit targets receive only one damage instance from that attack.
* Area damage inflicts firendly fire.

---

## Simultaneous Damage and Overkill

* All impacts resolved during the same simulation tick use the target state from the start of the combat-resolution step.
* Multiple attacks may therefore destroy each other during the same tick.
* Damage beyond the target's remaining health is overkill and does not transfer to another target.
* Experience credit uses effective damage and excludes overkill damage.

---

## Armor Classes

Initial armor classes:

* Light
* Medium
* Heavy
* Structure
* Air
* Capital

# Accuracy Rules

For the first prototype, use simple accuracy.

```text
Hit Chance = Base Accuracy - Movement Penalty + Veterancy Bonus
```

Recommended limits:

* Minimum hit chance: 40%
* Maximum hit chance: 100%

Initial rules:

* Stationary target: no penalty
* Moving target: -10%
* Fast target: -20%
* Veteran attacker: +5% to +15%

---

# Range Rules

Each weapon has:

* Minimum range if applicable
* Maximum range
* Line of sight requirement
* Target type restrictions

Artillery and siege weapons should outrange defenses but require protection.

---

# Targeting Rules

Units automatically prioritize targets based on role.

Example priorities:

1. Current command target
2. Immediate attacker
3. Best counter in range
4. Closest valid target

Players can override targeting with direct attack commands.

Additional targeting rules:

* Units do not automatically target enemies outside their acquisition radius.
* A direct attack target remains preferred while it is valid and within pursuit distance.
* If a target becomes hidden, direct-fire units stop attacking it.
* Unguided projectiles already in flight continue toward their assigned impact position.
* When an automatic target becomes invalid, the unit immediately searches for another valid target.
* Units automatically target neutral entities.

---

# Capture Rules

Outposts are captured by Envoy Units.

Capture process:

* Neutral defenders must be defeated first.
* Capturing unit must remain within capture radius.
* Capture takes 30 seconds.

---

# Outpost Specialization Rules

Available specializations:

* Industrial
* Energy / Research
* Military

Specialization should cost resources and take time.

Base rule:

* Cost: 500, 1000, 1500 Materials
* Time: 60 seconds

---

# Veterancy Rules

Units gain experience by participating in combat.

Experience sources:

* Dealing damage
* Destroying units
* Destroying buildings
* Capturing Outposts

Initial levels:

| Level | Name    | Bonus                            |
| ----: | ------- | -------------------------------- |
|     1 | Regular | No bonus                         |
|     2 | Veteran | +10% health, +5% accuracy        |
|     3 | Elite   | +15% damage, +10% health         |
|     4 | Heroic  | Unlocks special trait or ability |

Capital Units should have level instead of veterancy.

---

# Fog of War Rules

Each player has three visibility states:

* Unexplored
* Explored
* Visible

Rules:

* Units and buildings reveal nearby terrain.
* Enemy units are only visible in currently visible areas.
* Explored areas show terrain but not current enemy movement.
* Stealth or hidden units require detection.

Fog of war should make scouting important throughout the match.

---

# Defeat Rules

A player is defeated when they lose all critical command capability.

Initial rule:

A player loses when they have:

* No Command Structure
* No Construction Units
* No Outposts capable of rebuilding
* No production structures

This prevents instant defeat from losing only the starting base while still preventing endless hiding.

When a player is defeated:

* All remaining units owned by that player are immediately destroyed.
* All remaining buildings owned by that player become inactive and cannot attack, produce, research, detect, repair, or provide Energy.
* All production, construction, research, and command queues are cancelled.
* The defeated player can no longer issue gameplay commands.
* In team games, control of the defeated player's units is not transferred to allies.
* The defeated player may remain in the match as an observer.

---

# Stalemate Prevention

Frontier Command should avoid late-game defensive deadlocks.

Stalemate prevention systems:

## Valuable Contested Outposts

Central and forward Outposts provide strong strategic benefits.

## Siege Tools

Late-game units and research unlock tools for breaking defenses.

## Defense Limits

Defensive structures should delay attacks, not permanently stop them.

## Capital Units

Capital Units provide breakthrough potential.

---

# Design Goal

The gameplay rules should create a match where players must constantly choose between economy, expansion, research, and military power.

A player should win because they made better strategic decisions and executed decisive battlefield actions, not because they remained safely behind static defenses forever.
