# Frontier Command - Design Pillars

## Purpose

These pillars define the core principles of Frontier Command. Every major feature, mechanic, and design decision should support at least one of these pillars.

If a feature does not strengthen one or more pillars, it should be reconsidered or removed.

---

# Pillar 1: Battlefield First

The battlefield is the center of the player experience.

Players should spend most of their time commanding armies, expanding territory, and engaging enemy forces rather than managing distant locations or navigating multiple disconnected maps.

### Supports

* Tactical army control
* Base assaults
* Territorial expansion
* Battlefield positioning
* Frontline warfare

### Avoids

* Excessive empire administration
* Multi-layer strategic maps
* Long periods without combat
* Urge of passive gameplay

---

# Pillar 2: Satisfying Base Building

Building and expanding a base should be a meaningful part of gameplay.

Players should feel that they are establishing a military frontier that grows from a small outpost into a powerful war machine.

Base construction should provide strategic choices without becoming overwhelming.

### Supports

* Resource gathering
* Base expansion
* Defensive structures
* Production facilities
* Infrastructure upgrades
* Research structures

### Avoids

* Excessive micromanagement
* Complex city-building systems
* Large numbers of unnecessary structures

---

# Pillar 3: Strategic Progression

Players should become stronger through research, technology, and battlefield experience.

Strategic choices made during a match should shape how a faction develops and fights.

Progression should create meaningful decisions rather than simply increasing statistics.

### Supports

* Research trees
* Technology upgrades
* Unlockable abilities
* Specialized army compositions
* Alternative strategic paths

### Avoids

* Linear upgrade chains
* Mandatory research orders
* Research that provides only numerical bonuses

---

# Pillar 4: Special Units

Flagship-class units are valuable strategic assets that grow throughout a match.

These units should gain experience, unlock abilities, and become central to a player's strategy without completely overshadowing conventional armies.

### Supports

* Experience systems
* Level progression
* Ability unlocks
* Strategic preservation of important units

### Avoids

* Disposable hero units
* Overpowered "one unit wins everything" gameplay
* Mandatory flagship-focused strategies

---

# Pillar 5: Readable Large-Scale Warfare

Battles should feel large and impressive while remaining easy to understand.

Players should be able to quickly identify threats, opportunities, and battlefield conditions.

Victory should come from strategic decisions rather than visual confusion.

### Supports

* Clear unit roles
* Strong visual identity
* Predictable counters
* Readable battlefields
* Distinct faction silhouettes

### Avoids

* Visual clutter
* Excessive particle effects
* Unclear unit roles
* Hidden information
