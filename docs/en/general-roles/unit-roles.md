# Frontier Command - General Unit Roles

## Overview

General Unit Roles define the core battlefield functions that exist across all factions.

Every faction should have access to most battlefield roles, although each faction may fulfill those roles using different units and technologies.

These roles provide the foundation for army composition, counterplay, and strategic decision-making.

---

# Design Philosophy

Every unit should have a clear purpose.

Players should be able to understand:

* What a unit is good at.
* What a unit is weak against.
* How it contributes to an army.

No unit should be effective in every situation.

Army composition should be more important than unit quantity.

---

# Unit Categories

Unit categories describe the broad form of a unit, while unit roles describe its battlefield purpose. A single category may fulfill multiple roles depending on faction design and technology.

## Infantry

Common roles:

* Scouting
* Support operations

Infantry units are generally cheap and numerous.

## Light Vehicles

Common roles:

* Flanking
* Rapid response

Light vehicles are fast but fragile.

## Heavy Vehicles

Common roles:

* Frontline combat
* Assault operations
* Defensive warfare

Heavy vehicles form the backbone of most armies.

## Artillery

Common roles:

* Siege warfare
* Area denial
* Defensive bombardment

Artillery requires protection from faster units.

## Air Units

Common roles:

* Reconnaissance
* Precision strikes
* Rapid deployment

Air units provide strategic flexibility.

## Capital Units

Common roles:

* Battlefield leadership
* Strategic breakthroughs
* Force multiplication

Capital Units gain experience and level up throughout a match. They represent major strategic investments and should support conventional armies rather than replace them.

---

# Worker Unit

## Purpose

Provides construction and battlefield support.

## Responsibilities

* Build structures
* Repair buildings

## Characteristics

* Non-combat unit
* Essential for economic growth
* Vulnerable to attack

Every faction requires a Worker Unit.

---

# Envoy Unit

## Purpose

Capture unowned buildings

## Responsibilities

* Capture Structures
* Maybe do more like capture enemy units

## Characteristics

* Non-combat unit
* Essential for conquest
* Vulnerable to attacks

# Scout Unit

## Purpose

Gather information.

## Responsibilities

* Explore the map
* Locate enemy bases
* Monitor enemy expansion
* Detect stealth

## Characteristics

* Fast
* Low durability
* Low combat effectiveness

Information should be valuable throughout the entire match.

---

# Light Combat Unit

## Purpose

Early military presence.

## Responsibilities

* Defend expansion
* Raid enemy positions
* Support scouting
* Respond quickly to threats

## Characteristics

* Inexpensive
* Mobile
* Effective in groups

Forms the backbone of early-game armies.

---

# Main Battle Unit

## Purpose

Primary frontline combat force.

## Responsibilities

* Hold territory
* Engage enemy armies
* Form battle lines
* Support offensives

## Characteristics

* Balanced combat capability
* Moderate durability
* Reliable performance

This role forms the core of most armies.

---

# Heavy Assault Unit

## Purpose

Break enemy defenses and fortified positions.

## Responsibilities

* Lead assaults
* Destroy durable targets
* Absorb enemy fire

## Characteristics

* High durability
* High cost
* Lower mobility

Represents a significant military investment.

---

# Artillery Unit

## Purpose

Long-range battlefield support.

## Responsibilities

* Bombard defensive positions
* Support large battles
* Apply pressure from range

## Characteristics

* Very long range
* Fragile
* Requires protection

Critical for siege warfare.

---

# Anti-Armor Unit

## Purpose

Counter heavy vehicles and elite targets.

## Responsibilities

* Destroy armored threats
* Defend against assaults
* Support frontline units

## Characteristics

* High damage
* Specialized role
* Vulnerable when unsupported

---

# Anti-Air Unit

## Purpose

Protect forces from aerial threats.

## Responsibilities

* Destroy aircraft
* Defend infrastructure
* Secure airspace

## Characteristics

* Specialized weapon systems
* Limited effectiveness against ground targets

---

# Air Superiority Unit

## Purpose

Control the skies.

## Responsibilities

* Destroy enemy aircraft
* Protect friendly air units
* Provide aerial security

## Characteristics

* Fast
* High mobility
* Air-to-air specialization

---

# Strike Aircraft

## Purpose

Deliver precision attacks.

## Responsibilities

* Attack high-value targets
* Harass enemy infrastructure
* Support offensives

## Characteristics

* High burst damage
* Limited durability
* Requires protection

---

# Support Unit

## Purpose

Improve allied effectiveness.

## Responsibilities

* Repair units
* Provide buffs
* Supply intelligence
* Enhance battlefield performance

## Characteristics

* Limited direct combat ability
* Strong force multiplier

---

# Logistics Unit

## Purpose

Gather resources for the economy.

## Responsibilities

* Gather resources

## Characteristics

* Non-combat unit
* Requires protection

# Battlefield Counter Philosophy

Frontier Command should follow intuitive counter relationships.

Examples:

* Scouts defeat isolation through information.
* Light Units counter exposed support units.
* Main Battle Units defeat Light Units.
* Heavy Units defeat Light Units and Main Battle Units
* Anti-Armor Units defeat Heavy Units.
* Artillery defeats defensive positions.
* Air Units threaten Artillery.
* Anti-Air Units counter Air Units.

Counter systems should be understandable without requiring extensive memorization.

---

# Army Composition Philosophy

A successful army should contain multiple battlefield roles.

Players who rely on a single unit type should be vulnerable to counterplay.

The strongest armies combine:

* Frontline units
* Support units
* Specialized counters
* Siege capability
* Reconnaissance

Strategic diversity should be rewarded throughout all stages of the match.
