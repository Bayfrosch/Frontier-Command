# Frontier Command - Map & Territory Design

## Overview

Maps in Frontier Command are designed to support territorial expansion, strategic decision-making, and large-scale warfare.

The battlefield is not merely a location where armies fight. It is the primary strategic resource of the match.

Players expand their frontier by conquering territory, securing Outposts, and establishing military control over key regions.

The map should naturally create opportunities for conflict and prevent passive gameplay.

---

# Design Philosophy

## Territory Creates Power

Players become stronger by controlling more territory.

Expansion grants access to Outposts with specialisation for:

* Additional resources
* More energy and research
* Military strength

Territory should be valuable enough that players are encouraged to fight for it throughout the entire match.

---

## Expansion Through Military Action

Expansion should never be free.

To establish a new frontier, players must:

1. Build military forces.
2. Defeat neutral defenders.
3. Secure the region.
4. Defend it against enemy attacks.

Territory is earned through battlefield success.

---

## Frontlines Emerge Naturally

The game should not artificially create frontlines.

Instead, frontlines emerge as players expand into contested regions and compete for strategic positions.

A shifting frontier is a core element of Frontier Command's identity.

---

# Outposts

## Overview

Outposts are strategic objectives distributed across the battlefield.

All Outposts begin the match under neutral control and are protected by neutral defensive forces.

Before an Outpost can be captured, players must defeat its defenders.

---

## Purpose

Outposts serve multiple functions:

* Encourage expansion
* Create military objectives
* Establish territorial control
* Support economic development
* Create strategic choices

Outposts are intended to be among the most important locations on the map.

---

## Capture Process

1. Neutral defenders are defeated.
2. The Outpost becomes claimable.
3. A player captures the Outpost.
4. The player selects a specialization.

Once specialized, the Outpost becomes part of the player's territorial network.

---

# Outpost Specialization

Every Outpost begins as a neutral and generic structure.

After capture, players choose how the Outpost contributes to their strategy.

This allows players to adapt territory to their needs instead of relying on predefined map roles.

---

## Industrial Outpost

Focuses on material production and industrial development.

Benefits may include:

* Increased material income
* Improved harvesting efficiency

Best suited for:

* Economic expansion
* Large military production
* Resource-intensive strategies

---

## Energy Outpost

Focuses on energy generation and technological infrastructure.

Benefits may include:

* Increased energy production
* Improved research efficiency
* Support for advanced facilities

Best suited for:

* Technology-focused play
* Capital unit development
* Advanced military systems

---

## Military Outpost

Focuses on territorial control and force projection.

Benefits may include:

* Stronger local defenses
* Faster and cheaper Unit production
* Regional military bonuses

Best suited for:

* Frontline regions
* Chokepoints
* Defensive borders

---

# Map Generation

## Overview

Frontier Command supports procedural map generation.

Instead of relying solely on handcrafted maps, players select a map template and generate a battlefield using a random seed.

This provides replayability while maintaining strategic fairness.

---

# Generation Principles

## Symmetric Opportunity

Starting locations should provide comparable opportunities.

Players may begin in different terrain layouts, but available resources and expansion potential should remain balanced.

---

## Valuable Contested Regions

Maps should contain highly valuable territory that naturally attracts conflict.

These regions often become the center of major battles.

---

## Meaningful Terrain

Terrain should influence strategy without completely restricting movement.

Examples include:

* Rivers
* Mountain ranges
* Forests
* Urban environments
* Open plains

Terrain should create opportunities rather than dictate outcomes.

---

# Map Templates

Players choose a map template before generation.

Each template represents a way of resource distribution:

Examples:
R: Resource, P: Player, O: Outpost


1.
R - P - O - O - O - P - R


2.
    O
    |
P - O - P
    |
    O

---

# Desired Player Experience

Players should feel that every match tells a unique story.

A player begins with a small foothold, expands into dangerous territory, defeats neutral defenders, specializes captured Outposts, establishes a growing frontier, and ultimately fights decisive battles for control of the battlefield.

The map should be an active participant in the match rather than a passive backdrop.
