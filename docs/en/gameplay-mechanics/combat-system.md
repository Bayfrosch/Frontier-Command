# Frontier Command - Base Building, Army Building & Combat

## Overview

Frontier Command focuses on territorial warfare, military expansion, and large-scale battlefield command.

Players are not restricted by artificial construction ranges. Instead, expansion is determined by military control of the battlefield.

To establish a new frontier, players must clear neutral defenses, secure territory, and defend their newly claimed positions against enemy attack.

---

# Base Building

## Philosophy

Bases represent military infrastructure rather than fixed city-like settlements.

Players should be free to establish new bases wherever they can secure and defend territory.

Expansion is limited by military strength, not by construction range.

---

# Construction System

Players begin the match with a Construction Yard.

The Construction Yard serves as the starting command center and produces Construction Units.

Construction Units are responsible for building all structures.

Functions:

* Construct buildings
* Repair structures
* Establish forward positions
* Support expansion

Losing Construction Units should slow expansion and rebuilding efforts.

Protecting them remains important throughout the match.

---

# Building Placement

Players may place structures anywhere on the battlefield, no building radius or construction zone exists.

The only requirement is that a Construction Unit can physically reach the construction location.

This creates a highly flexible expansion system.

The challenge is not where a player is allowed to build, but where they can safely maintain control.

---

# Outposts

Outposts are strategic locations scattered throughout the map.

They are initially controlled by neutral defensive forces.

Players must defeat the neutral defenders before claiming the Outpost.

---

# Purpose of Outposts

Outposts create natural expansion objectives.

They serve several purposes:

* Secure territory
* Increase economic output
* Provide strategic positions
* Create frontlines
* Encourage conflict

Because Outposts are protected by neutral forces, players cannot expand uncontested.

Expansion requires military investment and time to defeat them.

---

# Frontier Expansion

As players capture Outposts they establish a network of military strongholds.

These strongholds become:

* Economic centers
* Defensive positions
* Staging grounds for attacks
* Reinforcement hubs

The frontier continuously moves as players gain or lose territory.

This reinforces the central theme of Frontier Command.

---

# Army Building

## Philosophy

Armies should feel large, powerful, and diverse.

The battlefield should contain enough units to create impressive engagements while still allowing players to understand what is happening.

The target scale is approximately:

* Early Game: 10-20 units
* Mid Game: 20-50 units
* Late Game: 50-150 units

This places Frontier Command between traditional RTS games and massive-scale strategy games.

---

# Army Composition

Victory should depend on army composition rather than pure numbers.

Every unit should have a clear battlefield role.

A balanced army is generally stronger than a force composed of a single unit type.

Players should constantly adapt their composition based on:

* Enemy technology
* Enemy unit composition
* Map control
* Strategic objectives

# Combat

## Philosophy

Combat should be fast, responsive, and decisive.

Players should be rewarded for:

* Positioning
* Army composition
* Strategic preparation
* Timing

The battlefield should always remain the primary focus of gameplay.

---

# Frontline Warfare

Combat should naturally create frontlines.

Players push into contested territory through:

* Capturing Outposts
* Establishing forward bases
* Destroying enemy defenses
* Securing resource regions

Frontlines should constantly shift as battles are won and lost.

---

# Defensive Structures

Defenses serve to support armies rather than replace them.

Defensive structures should:

* Delay attacks
* Weaken enemy forces
* Protect important locations

Defenses should not create permanent stalemates.

No position should be impossible to break.

---

# Siege Warfare

Late-game players gain access to advanced tools capable of breaking fortified positions.

Examples:

* Artillery
* Siege vehicles
* Capital units
* Specialized research

These tools ensure that the game continues progressing toward a conclusion.

---

# Decisive Battles

The late game should culminate in large-scale engagements involving hundreds of units.

Players who successfully balanced:

* Economy
* Expansion
* Research
* Army production

Should gain significant advantages.

However, victory is ultimately determined on the battlefield.

The final phase of a match should feature major offensives, breakthrough operations, and decisive battles that determine control of the frontier.

---

# Desired Player Experience

"I expanded beyond my starting position, defeated neutral forces to secure territory, built a network of frontier strongholds, assembled a powerful army, and won through superior battlefield command."
